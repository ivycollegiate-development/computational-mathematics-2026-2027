# Unit 0 — Course Survey

**Duration:** 2 days (1 paper survey + 1 discussion/overview)
**Format:** T-Th or first two days of class

---

## Day 1 — Paper Survey

**Objective:** Gauge math comfort, programming exposure, and security awareness.

**Materials:** Printed Survey-Doc-v1.0.md (one per student)

**Agenda:**
1. **Welcome (5 min)** — Quick intro: who Mr. Jones is, what this class is about
2. **Survey (25 min)** — Students complete the paper survey independently
3. **Collect & discuss (15 min)** — Ask for volunteers to share one interesting answer. Note: no right or wrong answers here.

**Teacher notes:** Collect surveys and review before Day 2. Sort into broad groups: no-code, some-code, comfortable — this shapes how fast you move through Unit 1.

---

## Day 2 — What is Computational Mathematics?

**Objective:** Students can explain what "computational math" means and why math matters for security.

**Materials:** Whiteboard, projector (show the course repo README)

**Agenda:**
1. **Review survey themes (5 min)** — "Here's what the class looks like overall..."
2. **What is Comp Math? (10 min)** — Whiteboard brainstorm: "What happens when you make math doable by a computer?"
   - Key idea: Computers are fast at arithmetic. Humans are good at strategy. Comp math is where they meet.
   - Historical touch: Babbage, Lovelace, Turing — computing was math before it was engineering.
3. **Tour the year (10 min)** — Walk through the 7 units and projects on the README. Show them where they're going.
   - Highlight one project from each unit quickly. "By the end, you'll build a cipher tool and a risk simulator."
4. **Why security? (10 min)** — Brief discussion: "Can you think of a time bad math caused a security problem?"
   - Examples: Therac-25 (overconfidence in software), Patriot missile rounding error (floating point), 747 MAX MCAS (sensor math gone wrong)
   - "Math isn't just about getting the right answer — it's about knowing when your answer could be weaponized."
5. **Toolchain preview (5 min)** — Show Google Classroom + GitHub Codespaces on the projector. "We start coding next week. Today was about where we're going."

**Homework:** None. (Or optional: write 3 sentences about what you're most curious about this year.)

## Teacher Reference — Suggested Follow-Up

| Profile | Approach in Unit 1 |
|---------|-------------------|
| No Python experience | Start with REPL, focus on concepts, T-Th paper reflections |
| Some Python experience | Code-alongs with challenges, pair programming |
| Comfortable with Python | Independent work, peer tutoring, stretch challenges |

Use survey Section 2 (question 4-5) to sort students into these rough groups.
